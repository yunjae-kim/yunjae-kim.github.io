Research & Projects

Thermal Stability Analysis of MOSFETs at Cryogenic Temperatures

Nov 2025- Present

Analysing of temperature dependence in metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistor (MOSFET) performance. When cooled below 298 K, the threshold voltage \(V_{th}\) increases due to Fermi level shift, while carrier mobility \(\mu\) improves from reduced phonon scattering. This creates a zero temperature coefficient (ZTC) bias point where the effects cancel, allowing thermally stable operation without external compensation.

Mentor: Su Huyn Lee — School of Energy, British Columbia Institute of Technology.

Effects of Anharmonicity on the Heat Capacity of Elemental Solids at High Temperatures

Oct 2025 - Present

In the study of crystals, many models account for only the harmonic behaviour of atoms in the lattice. A textbook example is the Debye model, which assumes atoms in the crystal only interact via a harmonic \(x^2\) potential. While this leads to correct low-temperature predictions, it fails at higher temperatures to predict correct material properties such as heat capacity. In this article, we investigated the effect of anharmonicity in the interatomic potential on the deviation of a material's heat capacity from the standard prediction. Moreover, we show that anharmonic effects can be used to quantitatively predict the high-temperature heat capacity of simple crystals using readily available material data. To obtain a correction, we modeled interatomic interactions with a Morse potential, which exhibits anharmonic behavior, and used this to relate the anharmonic coefficient to physical properties such as bond dissociation energy. Numerical analysis of several elemental solids showed strong agreement between predicted and fitted coefficients, indicating that anharmonic effects can be estimated directly from material parameters. This approach also demonstrates that the strength of anharmonic corrections is inversely proportional to bond dissociation energy, meaning weaker interatomic bonds lead to stronger deviations from harmonic behavior.

Mentor: Daniel Stedman — Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of British Columbia.

Quantum LATTE

Jan 2026 - Apr 2026

A 2D molecular dynamics simulation of argon atoms interacting via the Lennard-Jones potential, built to compute the phonon density of states from first principles. The simulation propagates 64 atoms using the Velocity Verlet algorithm under periodic boundary conditions, then extracts vibrational mode structure by Fourier transforming the velocity autocorrelation function (VACF), a classical application of the Green-Kubo relations.

GitHub: github.com/yunjae-kim/Quantum-LATTE

Symmetric VSEPR Geometries from Energy Minimization on a Sphere

Dec 2025 - Feb 2026

The basic VSEPR molecular geometries are derived by treating electron domains as identical repelling charges constrained to a sphere. In order to minimize the total Coulombic repulsion under the constraint, Lagrange multipliers and the Gram matrix are used show to that the minimum-energy configurations for \(N=2,3,4\) are linear, trigonal planar, and tetrahedral.

ChemRxiv: doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15000129/v1

Modelling the Behaviour of Ideal Gases

Dec 2024 - Jan 2025

Small simulation project using Unity and C# to model ideal gas behavior under varying thermodynamic conditions. Used the ideal gas law (\(PV=nRT\)) and the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution to replicate the random motion of particles. Created a visualization system showing particle dynamics and relationships between state variables.
